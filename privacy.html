Privacy Policy

Crystal Grimoire · last updated 19 November 2026

Crystal Grimoire is a game for Android made by Adam Plummer, a sole developer in the United Kingdom. This page explains what the game collects, why, who handles it, and how to have it deleted. In this policy, "I" and "me" mean Adam Plummer, who is responsible for your data (the "controller" under UK data protection law).

  • You don't make an account, and the game never asks for your name, email or contacts. Signing in with Google Play Games is optional and only backs up your game.
  • Crash reports are sent so I can fix the game.
  • Play data is sent only if you say Yes when the game asks, and you can turn it off any time in Settings.
  • Ads are shown only when you choose to watch one for a gift. They come from Google AdMob.
  • Remove Ads is bought through Google Play, and the game never sees your payment details. Your data is never sold.

What the game collects, and why

Crash and error reports

If the game crashes, freezes or hits an error, a report is sent to Unity's Diagnostics service so I can find and fix the problem. A report contains:

  • what went wrong (the technical details of the crash or error) and the game's last few log messages;
  • the phone's make and model, its processor, graphics chip, memory and screen size, the Android version, and the phone's language;
  • an approximate location (country and region) worked out from your internet address;
  • a random ID for this installation of the game, and the game's version.

Why: to keep the game working. This is my legitimate interest in fixing faults, and the reports don't identify you by name.

A random player ID

When the game connects to the internet, it signs in anonymously with Unity's Authentication service. This creates a random player ID for this installation; there is no email, name or password. The game uses it to fetch its settings from the server (below) and, if you sign in with Play Games, to keep your saved game.

Why: the services below can't work without it. This is my legitimate interest in running the game.

Game settings from the server

As it starts, the game asks Unity's Remote Config service for any balance changes (for example, how long the Lantern burns). The request carries the random player ID and the game's version, and nothing comes back but numbers for the game.

Why: so I can fix the game's balance without everyone needing an update. This is my legitimate interest in running the game.

Signing in with Google Play Games (optional)

If you use Google Play Games on your phone, the game can sign you in with it, so a new phone or a reinstall carries on with the same game. When you sign in, Unity's Authentication service links your Play Games account to the game's random player ID and stores your Play Games player ID with it. The game also saves a private copy of your game (your crystals, nights, Starlight, the story so far and the rest of your progress) to Unity's Cloud Save, so it can be restored on another phone. Only you can read that copy. The game doesn't see your email address or your Google password; the sign-in itself is handled by Google under Google's privacy policy.

Why: to keep your progress when you change or reset your phone. This is based on your choice to sign in; if you never sign in, nothing in this section happens.

Google user data

This section sets out exactly how Crystal Grimoire accesses, uses, stores and shares data from your Google Account when you sign in with Google Play Games.

  • What is accessed: your Google Play Games player ID, your Play Games gamer name, and a one-time sign-in code that Google issues for this game. The game asks for no other Google permissions: it cannot read your email address, contacts, Google Drive, photos or anything else in your Google Account.
  • How it is used: the player ID links your Play Games account to the game's own random player ID, so the same saved game comes back on a new phone or after a reinstall, and it lets the game unlock and show your Play Games achievements. The gamer name is shown only to you, in the game's Settings. The one-time code is passed straight to Unity's Authentication service to complete the sign-in and is not kept.
  • How it is stored: Unity's Authentication service stores your Play Games player ID alongside the game's player ID, and the private copy of your game is kept in Unity's Cloud Save, readable only by your account. Your gamer name is held on your phone only.
  • How it is shared: only with Unity Technologies, which processes it on my behalf to run the sign-in and Cloud Save. Your Google user data is never sold, never used for advertising or to choose ads, never shared with AdMob or any other ad network, and never shown to other players.
  • How to remove it: use Settings > Delete my account in the game, or see Delete your account, and the link and your Cloud Save copy are deleted. You can also stop the game using your Play Games account at any time in your Google Account under Third-party apps and services, or in the Play Games settings on your phone.

Crystal Grimoire's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Play data (only if you say Yes)

The game asks once whether it can send how your nights go. Only if you say Yes, it sends Unity Analytics:

  • during your first days, when you reach each early step (each of the opening story cards, your first tap, your first familiar, the second crystal waking, the Lantern, the first night, and coming back on another day), with how long you had played by then;
  • each night you hold, which night it is, how many Eclipses you've passed and how many hours you've played;
  • when you watch an ad to the end, which gift it was for and how many you've watched;
  • when the game offers Remove Ads, whether you chose to buy it, and when it's bought.

Unity also records basic session information: when the game starts and how long it runs, the phone model and Android version, an approximate country from your internet address, and the installation's random ID.

Why: to see where the game is too slow or too hard, and where new players stop. This is based on your consent. You can change your answer at any time in Settings > Privacy choices; turning it off stops anything more being sent.

Ads (only when you choose to watch one)

The game offers an ad in return for a gift: doubling the Mana made while you were away, the Moonlit boost, and the daily gift. An ad is only ever shown when you tap to watch one; there are no ads anywhere else in play. If you don't want to watch, nothing else in the game changes.

Ads are provided by Google AdMob. To choose, show and count ads, and to stop fraud, Google's ads software in the game collects:

  • your phone's advertising ID (a resettable ID that Android provides for ads);
  • your internet address and an approximate location (country and region) worked out from it;
  • the phone's make and model, Android version, language and screen size, and the game's name and version;
  • how you interact with the ads you watch (for example, whether an ad was watched to the end, so the gift can be given).

Because the game may be played by teenagers, it asks for non-personalised ads only for players in the UK, the European Economic Area and Switzerland, and for players in the United States (the game goes by the country of your phone's mobile network or SIM, or else your phone's region setting; it never reads your location). Non-personalised ads are chosen from things like the game itself and your approximate location, not from your interests or what you do in other apps. Elsewhere, Google may personalise ads using your advertising ID. On any Android phone you can reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalised ads, in your phone's Settings under Google > Ads (or Privacy > Ads).

Where the law requires your consent for ads, Google's consent message asks for it inside the game, and Settings > Privacy choices offers a way to change your choice.

Google uses this data under its own privacy policy, as an independent controller. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services and Google's privacy policy.

Why: the ads pay for the game, which is free to play. Rewarded ads are your choice each time.

Remove Ads

Remove Ads is bought through Google Play, which takes the payment under its own terms; the game never sees your card or bank details. The game receives a receipt for the purchase (which item, and Google's order reference), checks it, and records on your phone that you own it. Each time it starts, it asks Google Play what you own, so the purchase comes back after a reinstall. Once you own it, the game doesn't start Google's ads software at all.

Why: to give you what you paid for. This is necessary to perform the purchase you made.

Ratings

Twice at most (after your fifth night, and after the ending), the game asks Google Play to show its own rating sheet. Google decides whether it appears, and a rating or review you leave goes to Google Play under Google's terms; the game never sees it.

Kept on your phone only

Your game, its backups and your settings stay on your phone and are not sent anywhere, unless you sign in with Play Games (above). The reminder that the Lantern has burned down is set on your phone and sent by your phone; nothing about it goes to a server. Uninstalling the game deletes all of these.

Who handles your data

Crash reports, the player ID, play data, game settings and the Play Games copy of your game go to Unity Technologies, which provides these services and processes the data on my behalf. Unity may store and process it in the United States and other countries, with safeguards for international transfers as described in Unity's privacy policy.

Ad data goes to Google, as described under Ads above. Google may store and process it in the United States and other countries.

I don't sell your data, and nothing the game collects for crash reports or play data is used for advertising.

How long it is kept

  • Crash and error reports: for a limited period set by Unity, after which they are deleted.
  • Your player ID, your Play Games link and the cloud copy of your game: until you delete your account.
  • Play data: for as long as it helps me balance the game, within Unity's own retention limits.
  • Ad data: kept by Google under Google's own retention periods; I don't receive it.

Children

Crystal Grimoire is made for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at children under 13. I don't knowingly collect data from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game, contact me and I will delete what is held for their installation.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask for a copy of your data, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, object to how it is used, and withdraw consent for play data at any time (in Settings). For ads, you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's Settings, and change your ad consent in the game's Settings where it is offered. To delete your account and its data, use Settings > Delete my account in the game, or see Delete your account. For any other request, email crystalgrimoire.game@outlook.com. I will reply within one month.

If you're unhappy with how your data is handled, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts collecting anything new, for example when a new feature sends something it doesn't today, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
